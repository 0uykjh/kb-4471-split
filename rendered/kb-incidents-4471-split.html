#4471 · three problems, not one

owner Jakub Wilczek accepted reviewed 2026-10-30 · 0 days ago

#4471 was built by decision 0001: a report joins an open ticket when its subject matches, and nobody reads the body. People whose dates come out wrong write the same subject whatever went wrong, so the ticket grew into a bag of everything that makes a date look wrong.

Reading the bodies splits it. Each problem below has its own mechanism and its own fix. The parser stays in #4471, which was always its ticket. The other two moved out to tickets of their own, and their reports moved with them.

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October: the parser is fixed (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales), and the hint won’t be fixed because the booking-v3 form is retired. The one-day shift is the only problem still open.

parser — the shared parser reads dates month first

The one the desk already knew. Our tenants write the day first, the parser reads the month first, so a date comes back with its halves exchanged. The bodies say so outright: a booking made for one date arrives as the date you get by swapping its two numbers.

hint — booking-v3 prints the wrong date hint

causes.ts has always separated this from the parser, and the page about #4471 shows it in its own row, marked as belonging to no ticket of its own. Under the date field booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. Day-first tenants read the hint, type the month first as told, and the form reads what they typed their own way. The dates are wrong for the opposite reason: here the person followed the product, and the product changed its mind.

Fixing the parser does not fix this one. It would leave these tenants typing month first into a form that has started reading day first, which is the same wrong date from the other side.

clock — the booking keeps its date and moves one day

The one nothing in the kb could see. These bodies do not describe a swap. They describe a booking that slid by a single day:

  • an invite for Tuesday that the calendar shows on the Monday;
  • bookings that are one day off in June, and only in June;
  • the same meeting reaching Lisbon on Tuesday and Toronto on Monday;
  • an invite that arrived before the booking that caused it.

A date built in one timezone and read in another does exactly this, and a summer-only version of it is what a daylight-saving offset looks like. The desk saw these: desk-notes.json lists them under oneDayMove, and the #4471 page footnotes them. They were still counted under the parser, because the footnote never changed where they were filed.

Why the third one hid

causes.ts decides from the channel and the tenant. Those are what intake records, and they are enough to tell the parser from the hint, because that difference is a property of the form. They are not enough for this one. A one-day shift and a month flip reach the desk looking identical: the same twelve subject lines, the same tenants, the same channels. The difference is only in the words, and no rule in the kb reads the words.

Intake’s own guess is in the table above, and it is wrong often enough to be worth nothing as evidence. It is taken from the reporter’s browser, which is not where the bug is.

What to do with this page

  • Count a report under the problem its body describes, not the ticket its subject landed in.
  • A footnote is not a count. If reports do not belong to a ticket, move them.
  • Decision 0001 buys triage speed and pays for it in merged-together bugs. That trade is still the desk’s to make, but it should be made knowing a merged ticket can hold a bug nobody has named.
